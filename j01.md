## Journal 1 Entry: Rostock Max V2 3d Printer

Recently I got my hands on a Rostock Max V2, which is a large delta 3d printer. I received this as an unprompted gift, and have never used a 3d printer in my life. It came used & pre-assembled, with no instruction manual & a missing power cable.

The first thing I had to do was to buy the power cable to actually use it & turn it on. However, I had no instruction manual of any kind, and this printer was made back in 2014, in a time when 3D printing wasn't quite as beginner friendly as it is now. Therefore a lot of the official documentation was high-level & complex, with a lot of assumptions about prior knowledge leaving me, as a beginner, rather confused. Also since it was back in 2014 there was not a lot of documentation to even find.

After getting the power cable and flicking the power switch in the back, it flickered on, the fans kicking in and the blue display on the printer lighting up. However, after a quick test, the screen wasn't a touch screen and the only other interface was a dial I saw next to the screen. Turning the dial caused the screen to change but couldn't select anything. I tried firmly pressing the dial down to see if that would work but alas, nothing happened. This then prompted a look at the official documentation I could find, and after almost an hour of searching, I realized the dial *was* in fact a button, I just didn't hold it. 

After that mishap I then had to actually then print something on it. Looking to the side of the dashboard I saw an SD card inserted into the side, and after some more research knew that this was how the prints got from the PC onto the printer. So I then plugged the SD card into my computer, downloaded a model, and then realized I didn't know what the proper way to convert the file was. After *more* research I came across slicers, programs that converted the 3d model into instructions for the printer in a .gcode file. I used the slicer Cura, since that is what the previous owner used. 

After finally doing all of that, I downloaded a little test print, and started it. It said it would take 2 hours, so I went out with my roommates, and came back the print failed, with the error of **Heater Decoupled**. That was it, no further elaboration, and the documentation was supremely unhelpful. So I solved it very simply. With brute force testing, due to the fact I refused to believe it was hardware failure because then I could not actually print anything. And after several hours of trial and error, I eventually figured out that the issue was that there were aftermarket fans installed, and they cooled the extruder (the part that melts the filament) faster than it could heat, and the temperature failing to reach heat caused the error. 

The solution was then going into the settings & forcing the fans to run at most 40% speed. There were a host of other minor configuration issues I figured out through much more trial and error, but this was the process of learning how to actually understand the printer.
